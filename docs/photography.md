The Tikri Project relies heavily on photographs as primary source material for documenting places, buildings, objects, documents, and everyday life in Tikri. This page provides guidance on taking photographs that are useful for documentation, archival preservation, storytelling, and book production.

The aim is not to produce professional or artistic photography, but to create clear, accurate, and well-documented visual records that can support publications, community archives, and future research. Most photographs for the project are expected to be taken using ordinary smartphones and modest technological resources.

These guidelines are intended for project participants, contributors, and others who may wish to reproduce similar community documentation projects using the Tikri methodology.

## Most Important Rules

Before uploading photographs to the Tikri Project, contributors should ensure that:

* EXIF metadata is preserved. Check that the camera application is recording available metadata such as date and time.
* Important subjects are photographed in both portrait and landscape orientation where practical.
* Original photographs are retained and never overwritten.
* Edited versions are saved as separate files.
* Photographs are transferred using methods that preserve the original image quality and metadata.
* Screenshots are not used when the original image file is available.
* Photographs are reviewed before leaving a location to ensure they are in focus and usable.
* Important photographs are backed up before being deleted from a device.

## General Principles

Photographs for the Tikri Project should prioritise documentation rather than artistic expression. The objective is to create accurate visual records that help preserve information about places, buildings, objects, documents, and everyday life in Tikri.

Contributors should focus on clarity, context, and usefulness rather than special effects, filters, or advanced photographic techniques. A photograph should help future readers understand what existed, where it existed, and how it appeared at a particular point in time.

Whenever possible, contributors should take multiple photographs of a subject, including wider views that provide context and closer views that capture important details.

## What to Photograph

The project may document:

* Homes and buildings.
* Streets, lanes, and public spaces.
* Shops and commercial establishments.
* Religious and community structures.
* Objects and artefacts.
* Documents and printed material.
* Signboards, notices, and inscriptions.
* Historical or culturally significant locations.
* Infrastructure such as roads, wells, water tanks, and public facilities.

Contributors are encouraged to photograph both notable and ordinary subjects, as seemingly routine features can become valuable historical records over time.

## What Not to Photograph

The current focus of the Tikri Project is the documentation of places, buildings, streets, public spaces, objects, artefacts, documents, printed material, and other aspects of the physical environment of Tikri.

The following are currently outside the primary scope of this guidance:

* People.
* Animals.

## Taking Documentary Photographs

Photographs should be in focus, well lit, and clearly framed.

Where practical, photographs should record both the subject itself and its surroundings. Context often provides information that may not be apparent from a close-up image alone.

Contributors should avoid excessive editing, filters, or effects that alter the appearance of the subject.

Whenever possible:

* Take multiple photographs of important subjects.
* Capture both wide views and close-up details.
* Ensure important signs, inscriptions, and objects are fully visible.
* Review photographs before leaving a location.

## File Naming

Photograph filenames should be descriptive and should include the subject and date.

Examples:

* `tikri-market-2026-06-01-original.jpg`
* `tikri-market-2026-06-01-edited.jpg`
* `tikri-water-tank-2026-06-01-original.jpg`
* `tikri-school-building-2026-06-01-original.jpg`

Avoid automatically generated filenames such as:

* `IMG_4821.jpg`
* `DSC00125.jpg`

## Preserving Original Photographs

Contributors may edit photographs for publication, but the original unedited file should always be retained.

For example:

* `tikri-market-2026-06-01-original.jpg`
* `tikri-market-2026-06-01-edited.jpg`

The original file serves as the archival master copy and should not be overwritten.

## Recording Information

Contributors should record relevant information while memories remain fresh. Useful information may include:

* Location.
* Date.
* Description of the subject.
* Historical, cultural, or community significance.
* Any names, dates, or inscriptions visible in the photograph.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

* Blurred photographs.
* Poor lighting.
* Incomplete views of the subject.
* Obstructed signs, documents, or objects.
* Excessive use of filters or image editing.
* Failure to preserve original files.
* Failure to record contextual information.
* Taking only a single photograph of an important subject.

Contributors should review photographs before leaving a location whenever possible and retake images if necessary.
